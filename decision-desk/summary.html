Executive summary

What is ready, what is blocked, what to decide

Two minutes of reading: the numbers that survived verification, the numbers that could not be verified at all, and the order in which decisions should be taken.

Ready and verified. 136 official 2026 finals captured and cross-checked against official standings (154 of 160 team fields reproduce exactly; the six that do not belong to the one game the NFL publishes with a hidden score). League scoring constants, opponent-adjusted ratings, home-field estimate, split-half reliability, three measured correlations, a walk-forward skill test, a 254-row injury capture and a 119-row injury-to-depth join are all reproducible from scripts in this folder.
Blocked. Licensed prices, historical props, settlement rules, confirmed participation and snap counts, reward funding and a precedence rule for conflicting official pages. Until those exist, no originated line can be called value, no strategy can pass the market gate, no prop correlation can be stated and no reward can be paid.

The decision sequence I recommend

  1. Settle the five critical governance questions (product/legal, licensed prices, vig convention, settlement, precedence). Everything downstream depends on them.
  2. Freeze one canonical evidence chain and archive the six other workspaces as exploratory.
  3. Close the participation gap: without an official snap or participation source, the injury work answers "who is listed" but never "who played".
  4. Extend the game log to prior seasons before any rating is treated as more than exploratory.
  5. Only then re-open strategy promotion, with a pre-registered, multiplicity-corrected test.
What I deliberately did not do. I did not fill a single blank with an estimate, I did not copy a market price, I did not label a community dataset as official, and I did not claim an edge from a positive but insignificant skill score.
