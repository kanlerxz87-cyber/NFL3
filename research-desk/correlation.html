Correlation between legs

Parlays and same-game combinations are only worth pricing when the joint outcome is measured, not assumed. Every number here comes from 3031 settled games.

Method: each leg is graded as a settled binary outcome (cover, over/under, moneyline win). Dependence is the phi coefficient — Pearson correlation between the two 0/1 legs — with a 95% Fisher-z interval. Pushes are excluded from that pair.

The specific case that breaks naive parlay maths

A same-game parlay of home moneyline + home spread + over is the standard product offered on a game. Multiply the three single-leg probabilities and the combined chance is overstated. This table measures the first two legs directly.

What this means for pricing

  • Two legs in the same game must be priced with a joint probability, not a product. The lift factor above is the size of the correction found in this sample.
  • Legs in different games can be treated as independent for pricing, but the sportsbook's own parlay multiplier still applies its hold on top.
  • Player-prop combinations need player-level joint outcomes that are not in this snapshot. That is an open question with a named data requirement, published on the Questions page.
