Executive summary

WHAT THE DATA SUPPORTS

Availability is knowable. Impact is not.

The official injury report and the 30 captured club depth charts are enough to name, for each listed player, the published string behind him. That produced 195 replacement chains. It also produced 43 players who are on the league report but hold no published string at their position on their own club chart — for those there is no official next-man-up to name, and the cell is left empty rather than guessed.

WHAT BLOCKS EVERYTHING ELSE

Three gaps, and none of them are modelling gaps.

1. The source moves. Re-read the official injury page roughly sixteen hours after the committed capture and it had gained 25 rows. A single capture cannot support any availability feature. 2. Depth is published shallow. 500 of 676 position groups publish no third string, so a third- or fourth-string replacement is usually unknown. 3. There is no price history. Without a licensed odds archive nothing can be scored, so every strategy stays at "not run" by construction.

Worked example: the Patriots backfield

Three Patriots are ruled Out for Week 10. Their running-back chain is published four deep: Rhamondre Stevenson (1st), TreVeyon Henderson (2nd), Corey Kiner (3rd), Lan Larison (4th). Henderson is Out and Stevenson is on the same report as Did Not Participate, so the published next men are the 3rd and 4th strings. This is the case the brief asks for — and it is the only kind of case the data can answer. Whether Kiner or Larison plays, and what it costs, is not published by anyone.
