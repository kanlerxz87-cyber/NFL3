The brief's premise — that every club publishes four strings at every position — does not hold. Three groups publish a third string at more than half the clubs: running back (26), tight end (18) and linebacker (18). Only one club in thirty publishes a fourth-string cornerback, and no club publishes a fourth-string offensive lineman or edge rusher.

02 / THE VOCABULARY PROBLEM

Clubs do not publish a common set of position labels

A structural limit, not a parsing bug. Nine of the thirty captured clubs publish no player under any edge-rusher label (DE, LDE, RDE, EDGE or RUSH) — they publish "DL" and "OLB" instead. This workspace does not guess which of those is the edge rusher, so those cells stay blank with the reason shown. Filling them would mean inventing a depth order the club never published, which the brief explicitly rejects.

03 / SHARED AND EMPTY SLOTS

149 of 676 slots name more than one first string

The brief warns that treating column one as "the starter" would fabricate a starter the club never named. This workspace treats a slot as a group and never reduces it to a single named starter.
