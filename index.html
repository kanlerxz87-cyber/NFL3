Executive summary

This repository started with only a six-character README heading and no datasets, code, documented requirements, or model to audit. This page establishes a source-linked decision framework, review queue, and validation gates; it does not claim to produce profitable lines. A serious line-making system needs synchronized historical odds and results, independently constructed probabilities, official availability/depth-chart context, and prospective testing after accounting for vig, market movement, and dependence.

Important: the NFL injury page was inspected, but the returned snapshot presented a 2026 Week 10 slate with apparent roster/schedule inconsistencies. No player status or game information was imported. Confirm each dated official report at decision time.

Proposed promotion gates (thresholds must be agreed before seeing results)

1. Data and settlement audit → 2. Leakage-free chronological backtest → 3. Calibration and benchmark comparison → 4. Robustness across seasons/market conditions → 5. Prospective paper track → 6. Independent review → only then consider a limited leaderboard reward. Pre-register eligibility, minimum sample, scoring, ties, and disqualification rules to discourage luck, overfitting, and cherry-picking. A leaderboard cannot remove luck; confidence intervals and adequate sample sizes only quantify uncertainty.

Lineups, injuries & replacement depth

Track offense and defense separately. Each record should preserve player identifier, team, position, official report date, practice participation, game designation, inactive status, source URL, retrieval timestamp, and source text. Keep report history rather than overwriting it. A depth tier is not inferred from injury status: require a dated official team depth chart or other approved primary source.
