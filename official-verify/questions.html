Questions that must be answered before a decision

Thirty-five questions, each with what we know, what we do not, what it blocks, and the cheapest action that would close it. Every question is sourced or explicitly marked unobtainable.

What this page is. These are the questions that must be answered before a decision can reasonably be made, written so that each one names what is already known, what is missing, what it blocks and the cheapest action that would settle it. None of them is rhetorical: each has an action attached.
Read the "what it blocks" column as a priority list. The price question (Q01) blocks more downstream work than any other, because without prices there is no return, no closing-line value, and no residual against a market. Depth ordering (Q11) blocks the largest cluster of availability features.

Q14DepthCan 'starter' be defined without a depth chart or a snap count?Known: Both candidate sources are unavailable (OV-S14, OV-S15). Q15ModelIs the home-field term real and stable, or is it an artefact of a short sample?Known: The fitted home-field term on 137 official games is -0.49 points, i.e. the home team is slightly *less* favoured than neutral on average.Not known: Whether that sign is real, and how it behaves across seasons.A sign error in home field silently inverts every side. The number is published so it can be criticised, not assumed.Re-fit on a second season with the same registered method and report both.OV-S10 ↗
Q16ModelIs the registered model distinguishable from a coin flip on out-of-sample games?Known: On 89 out-of-sample games its Brier skill against the home base rate is +0.119, but the cluster-bootstrap 95% interval on the paired improvement is -0.0099 to +0.0691, so the lower bound is below zero and the gate fails.Not known: Whether the sign holds on a larger sample, which is a different season rather than more weeks of this one.A strategy cannot be promoted on a point estimate whose interval crosses zero. This is the honest answer and is published as such.Add a second season to the corpus and re-run the same frozen evaluation.OV-S01 ↗
Q17ModelIs a fixed margin standard deviation of 13.5 defensible, or should it come from the data?Known: 13.5 was fixed in advance precisely so the evaluation could not tune itself. The observed in-sample residual standard deviation of the per-team score is about 8.19 points.Not known: The marginal standard deviation of game margins in this corpus, and whether it is stable.A wrong sigma miscalibrates every probability, which changes the Brier score without changing the ranking.Publish the marginal margin distribution as a descriptive number and, in a *new* registration, allow sigma to be fitted per step.registration corpus ↗
Q18ModelHow much of the club win table is luck?Known: Using the registered model's own probabilities, the implied Bernoulli variance accounts for about 31% of the observed dispersion in club win totals, leaving about 69% unexplained by a coin flip.Not known: Whether 31% is stable, and how much of the remaining 69% is genuine skill versus model error.Rewarding a lucky staking run is the failure mode the brief names. A published luck share is the guard rail.Repeat the decomposition per season and publish the spread across seasons.OV-S10 ↗
Q19ModelIs the model calibrated, or only accurate on average?Known: Calibration is published in six probability bins over 89 games. Several bins hold fewer than ten games and are marked unreadable.Not known: Whether the mid-range bins have a systematic bias, which 89 games cannot resolve.A model that is calibrated nowhere except at the extremes cannot price a moneyline.Accumulate a second season and re-run the identical bins.OV-S01 ↗
Q20ModelDo injuries change outcomes enough to justify a feature, and by how much?Known: No dated availability series exists (Q08), so no injury feature is in any model here.Not known: The difference in out-of-sample skill between identical models with and without verified availability features.A fixed points penalty for a missing starter is a guess. The brief asks for an ablation with uncertainty, and the ablation needs the dated series first.Build the dated series (Q08), then run a chronological-holdout ablation with the same model twice.OV-B04 ↗
Q21ModelWhat is the actual margin distribution of this season's games, and is a Normal a reasonable shape?Known: The corpus supports the computation; it is published as a descriptive statistic rather than assumed.Not known: Fat tails in the empirical distribution, which matter for totals and for any extreme probability.A Normal tail understates blowouts, which misprices large spreads.Publish the empirical quantiles of margin and total beside the model's assumptions.OV-S07 ↗
Q22GovernanceWhat exactly qualifies as a 'validated' strategy, and who decides?Known: Two gates are registered (G-PREREG and G-CLUSTER) and the one scored model fails G-CLUSTER by its lower bound.Not known: Whether a business owner would accept a rule that leaves the leaderboard empty for a whole season.If the bar is lowered to fill the board, the board stops measuring skill.Write the acceptance rule into a governance note with a named decision-maker and a date, and do not change it mid-season.registration corpus ↗
Q23GovernanceShould the leaderboard rank on skill or on return, and can we even compute return?Known: Return cannot be computed: no price series exists (Q01). Skill can be, and is.Not known: Whether a future price source would change the ranking rule.A return-based leaderboard without prices is impossible; a win-rate leaderboard is misleading.Adopt benchmark-relative proper-score ranking as the default and state that return is a secondary, price-dependent column.OV-B01 ↗
Q24GovernanceAre parlays and prop combos admissible at all right now?Known: Multiplying marginal probabilities is invalid when legs are dependent. No joint probability has been validated and no operator void/push rules have been obtained.Not known: The dependence structure between legs, which requires joint historical outcomes at the leg level.A ranked combo without a validated joint probability is a fabricated number.Obtain leg-level historical outcomes and operator rules; only then estimate a joint model.OV-B01 ↗
