Leaderboard, calibration and the removal of luck

One registered strategy, three controls, and the luck decomposition. Proper scores only; no win-rate ranking and no return claim, because this repository holds no prices.

The gate fails, and that is the result. The registered model has a better Brier score than every control, but the uncertainty interval on its improvement crosses zero. Under the rule frozen before the run, that makes it unvalidated. It is shown here with its interval rather than removed, and it earns nothing.
Why not rank by win rate or return. A short sample of wins is mostly noise, and this repository holds no prices, so return cannot be computed at all. Ranking is therefore by benchmark-relative proper score with a clustered interval, and the two controls you asked for (random picks and favourites only) sit on the same board rather than in a footnote.

The removal of luck

Observed win equivalents (ties counted 0.5) across the 32 official clubs are compared with the wins the pre-registered model expected out of sample, and with the Bernoulli variance the model's own probabilities imply. The ratio of implied Bernoulli variance to observed variance of club win totals is the share of club-win dispersion that is luck rather than signal.

With these outcomes, 31.0% of the observed dispersion in club win totals is consistent with the Bernoulli variance implied by the model's own probabilities, leaving 69.0% unexplained by a coin flip. Observed variance 3.870; mean implied Bernoulli variance 1.200; n = 32 clubs.

This is not 'true skill'. It says how much of the spread in club win totals a coin-flip process could have produced. With one season and 32 clubs the estimate itself is uncertain, and it is conditional on the registered model.
