Capture

Each page is fetched over HTTPS from www.nfl.com only; a redirect to any other host is refused rather than followed. Bytes are stored content-addressed by SHA-256 and never overwritten. Every attempt — success, structural drift or transport failure — appends one line to injury-archive/data/index.jsonl with its requested and captured clock times, so the history is a log of reads, not a snapshot of conclusions.

Parser

The injury-report parser is imported from decision-lab/scripts/capture_official.py, the same parser that produced the 347-row Week 10 read already in this repository. Re-using it means the two paths cannot disagree about what the page says. The inactives parser walks the article in document order and refuses anything it does not recognise.

Join and estimator

Club and week are fixed; the player name is normalised (case, punctuation, parentheticals, whitespace) and matched exactly, then with a generational suffix ignored, then refused as ambiguous if two candidates remain. Rates use the Wilson score interval at 95% with z = 1.959964, and n is printed beside every value.

Pre-registration

The protocol was committed before the first result. Its text is reproduced from the repository file below; the commit that adds it is the registration.

The registration commit is the commit that adds this file. `injury-archive/scripts/verify.py` asserts that the commit exists and precedes the commit that added `injury-archive/data/designations.json`. """ # Injury-designation calibration — pre-registration (ID: IA-PRE-01) **Registered by:** session `arena/d37d5907-nfl3` **Registration date:** 2026-11-16 UTC **Registration commit:** the commit that adds this file (asserted by `injury-archive/scripts/verify.py`) ## 1. Question Does an official weekly game-status designation predict whether the player is on that week's official inactive list? The NFL rulebook and the Competition Committee's published revision define the labels but publish no percentage. This protocol measures the observed rate and its uncertainty rather than importing a remembered or third-party percentage. ## 2. Sources (both official, both required per observation) | Field | Source | |---|---| | designation, practice status, injury, club, player | `https://www.nfl.com/injuries/league/{season}/REG{week}` | | inactive list membership, club, player | `https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-week-{week}-inactives-players-ruled-out-sunday-{season}-season` | Each captured page is stored content-addressed under `injury-archive/data/raw/{sha256}.html.gz` and its retrieval clock time and digest are recorded in `injury-archive/data/index.jsonl`. ## 3. Population Every row of a captured weekly injury report whose *Game Status* cell is one of `Out`, `Doubtful`, `Questionable` — the three labels the official page publishes. Rows with a blank game-status cell are out of scope for this measurement (they are still published on the injury pages) because there is no designation to calibrate. Every reported exclusion is counted and published beside the rates. ## 4. Join Within one club and one week: * normalise the name by lower-casing, removing parenthetical text, removing every non-alphanumeric character except spaces, and collapsing whitespace; * an **exact** normalised match to an inactive list entry is a match; * if there is no exact match, a match that ignores a trailing generational suffix (`jr`, `sr`, `ii`, `iii`, `iv`, `v`) is accepted and labelled `suffix-insensitive`; * two or more candidate matches are `ambiguous` and are excluded, not resolved by choosing one; * a designation whose club does not appear in the inactives article is `uncovered` and excluded; * a designation with no match is `active` — the only reading the two documents support. The join key is a name and a club. It is not a player ID, and the protocol does not pretend otherwise: a same-club, same-week duplicate name would be ambiguous and is excluded by the rule above, and any residual mismatch is a bias towards *active* that the published counts expose. ## 5. Estimator and uncertainty For each designation `d` over the pooled population: * `n_inactives` = number of captured inactives articles in the population * `n` = joined designations for `d` * `k` = joined designations where the player is on the inactive list * point estimate `p = k / n` * 95% interval: Wilson score interval, `z = 1.959964` (two-sided) * `n` is published beside every value. A cell with `n = 0` is published blank, never as zero. Secondary cuts, published with the same rule and the same `n`: * by season; * by practice status (`Did Not Participate In Practice`, `Limited Participation in Practice`, `Full Participation in Practice`); * by designation rank (`Out` < `Doubtful` < `Questionable`); * emergency third quarterback entries counted separately, because the article labels them and they are inactive by rule rather than by injury. ## 6. Exclusion accounting (fail-closed) The page publishes, next to every rate: the number of designations with no captured inactives article, the number that were ambiguous, the number whose club was uncovered, and the number of captured inactives articles whose own parse recorded a structural failure. A rate is **not published** for any season × week pair whose two documents did not both parse cleanly. ## 7. Gates (all must pass before any rate is called a finding) 1. `G1` coverage: at least one season × week pair has both documents captured and clean. 2. `G2` accounting: `uncovered + ambiguous + active + inactive == total designations` for every cut. 3. `G3` interval: every published rate carries its Wilson interval and its `n`. 4. `G4` no back-fill: no row enters the population without both official URLs and digests. 5. `G5` stability: the rate is reported per season as well as pooled, and the page says so if the per-season intervals do not overlap. 6. `G6` no causal language: the statistic is a conditional rate on published documents, not an estimate of a player's probability of playing, which additionally depends on the game-day roster and on information the report does not contain. ## 8. What would change our mind (two-sided, frozen) * **Would confirm** the designation is a usable feature: for each of the three labels the lower bound of the pooled Wilson interval separates it from the next label's upper bound, with `n >= 100` per label, holding in the per-season cuts. * **Would falsify** it: overlapping intervals between adjacent labels, or a pooled ordering that inverts a per-season ordering, in which case the label is a weak ordinal and may not be used as a model feature; it would be replaced by the measured rate itself with its interval. ## 9. What this protocol does not claim It does not measure snaps, starter status, workload or replacement quality. Being inactive is not the same as being unavailable for the whole game, and a player who is active may take no snaps. Those questions need an official snap-count or participation source, which this repository has not verified (see `readiness.html` row A7).
