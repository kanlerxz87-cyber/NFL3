Backtest, correlations, luck and the weekly diff

Everything on this page was computed from games that had already finished. It exists to show whether the machinery separates skill from noise - it is not a leaderboard result and is never quoted as one.

Proper-score metrics

Read the lower bound, not the point estimate. CT-RAND - a coin flip - reaches a bootstrap p of 0.045 on this window while its skill interval still covers zero: that is what a leaderboard looks like when it is scored on a point estimate. No entry here has a positive lower bound, so nothing on this page is evidence of skill, and the sample-size column says how far away the corpus is from being able to say anything else.

Removal of luck

A hit count that matches the model's own expectation says nothing about whether the probabilities were good: the scoring detail says more. Here the realised outcomes were less predictable than the model's probabilities anticipated, which the calibration table below shows as over-confidence in the 0.6-0.8 band rather than as bad luck.
