Overview

Methodology

The model, its equations, its assumptions and its limits — small enough to audit in one sitting.

The model is deliberately small enough to check by hand. Every equation below is in scripts/model_ratings.py, and every published number is its output.

1. The rating

For club i with games played G:

off_i = PF_i / G def_i = PA_i / G r_i = (PF_i − PA_i) / G

An additive points model says a club's score is league average plus its own offence minus the opponent's defence. The margin then collapses to a difference of the two ratings:

points_A = μ + off_A − def_B margin_A = points_A − points_B = r_A − r_B

with μ the league average points per team per game (measured here at 22.963). Because a club with 8 games and a club with 9 games are not equally informative, each rating is shrunk toward zero:

r'_i = r_i × G / (G + k)

with k = 2 declared as assumption A-03. At 8 games this keeps 80% of the raw rating.

2. The two totals

The same additive model forces the total to equal the league total plus both ratings, which structurally pushes totals down when two weak clubs meet. Rather than hide that, two estimators are published:

total_additive = L + r'_home + r'_away total_average = ( (PF_h + PA_h)/G_h + (PF_a + PA_a)/G_a ) / 2

They disagree by up to 12.2 points on the verified slate. The gap is published as a column, not resolved by preference.

3. Win probability

P(home win) = Φ( margin_home / σ ), σ = 13.5 (assumption A-02)

σ is a parameter, not an estimate. It is used only to turn a margin into a probability and it is listed with the other assumptions.

4. Luck, and why it is a subtraction

The Pythagorean expectation converts scoring into a win rate. Its exponent is fitted here rather than borrowed: k minimises squared error against the actual win rates of the 32 clubs, giving k = 3.51.

pyth_i = PF_i^k / (PF_i^k + PA_i^k) expected_wins = pyth_i × G luck_wins = (W + 0.5·T) − expected_wins

Read that last line carefully: luck here is only the part of a record that scoring does not explain. It also contains real things the margin misses — special teams, opponent quality, clutch play. Treating all of it as luck would be an error, and the site says so wherever the number appears.

5. Correlations

Pearson r on the 32-club cross-section, with two-sided p-values via Fisher z. n = 32 is small; the p-values are reported so the reader can see that. Two results carry explicit warnings: C7 is a part-whole correlation and is mechanically inflated, and C8 is split-half reliability rather than a momentum effect.

7. What the model cannot do

  • No opponent adjustment. r_i credits a club for the quality of who it beat. Fixing this needs a game log (SG-02).
  • No market comparison. There is no line to compare against (F-04). A model number is not an edge.
  • No injury adjustment in the published lines. The injury layer exists and is verified, but the position-value weights that would connect it to a line are assumptions (A-04), so the two are published side by side rather than silently combined.
  • No neutral-site science. One verified game (New England at Detroit in Munich) is flagged neutral and the home-field term is forced to zero there (A-06).
