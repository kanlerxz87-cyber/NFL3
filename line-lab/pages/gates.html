Validation gates

Thirteen gates, five reward tiers, and the arithmetic that decides how much evidence is enough.

All arithmetic on this page is illustrative, not empirical

Every table here is computed from declared parameters — a break-even rate, a target edge, a correlation. None of it is an observed result from NFL data. The warning is printed in the generated JSON too, so it travels with the numbers.

The 13 gates

Ordered so that the cheapest disqualifiers run first. Gate G1 is the one that changes the design of the whole competition:

Detecting a 2-point edge over break-even at 80% power needs roughly 4,887 graded bets on the win/loss test and 19,623 on the ROI test. A 40-bet hot streak cannot clear it.

This is why the leaderboard cannot rank on a single season, and why closing-line value — which carries far more signal per bet — has to be the primary metric rather than realised profit.

Hard gates cannot be waived. A strategy failing any hard gate receives no reward, whatever its profit.

G9 — why independence pricing manufactures edge

Two legs at 55% each have a 30.25% joint chance if independent. If they move together at ρ = 0.6, the joint chance is 40.4% — a third higher. A book that priced them as independent would be offering a fair price of 3.31 when the true fair price is 2.48.

The price a book would post if it treated the legs as independent. Two same-game legs that move together are systematically under-priced by an independent-leg model, so the EV column is the SIZE of that pricing error, not a claim that such a price is on offer. Most books apply a correlation adjustment to same-game parlays; whether that adjustment is right is an empirical question and is exactly what Q-14 has to answer.
