Availability

Depth & availability

Verified availability degradation by position — and an explicit refusal to label anyone a starter or a backup without a source.

The requirement this page cannot yet fully meet

The brief asks for detection of 2nd, 3rd and 4th string players replacing starters, on offence and defence. That is a depth-order question, and the official NFL.com roster feed does not publish depth order. Its columns are player, number, position, roster status, height, weight, experience and college — verified by reading the page in full (S07, R14).

So this page does not claim who replaces whom. Any 'backup' or 'starter' label here would be an invented fact. What it does instead is measure the part that is verified: how much availability is degraded, by position group, for each club.

What the index measures

DepthStress = Σ ( position_weight × availability_weight )

Availability weight: 1.0 for Out or Did Not Participate, 0.9 for Doubtful, 0.6 for Questionable, 0.5 for Limited Participation, 0 for Full Participation. Position weights are declared in scripts/model_ratings.py and are an explicit assumption (A-04) — no official source publishes position value. The index is normalised to the highest club in this snapshot.

It is NOT a depth-chart measure. It says how much verified availability is degraded at a position, not who replaces whom. The official NFL.com roster feed exposes position and roster status but no depth order (R14), so '2nd/3rd/4th string' attribution cannot be verified and is left out.

How to close the gap

Route A — per-club depth charts

Every club publishes its own depth chart on its official site. They are not uniform, not machine-readable, and update on different cadences. It would need verification club by club, with a documented cadence, before a single 'starter' claim is published.

Route B — official snap counts

If an official snap-count source exists, participation share can rank players empirically without anyone publishing a depth chart. Unverified: no such source was fetched in this session (Q-03).

Open questions: Q-09 (source), Q-10 (position value), F-08 (flag). Suggested next step: SG-05, recommended as a pilot, not an immediate build.

Why the weighting is published rather than buried

A single index whose weights are hidden looks like a measurement. This one is deliberately parameterised: change the weight table in the script and the ranking changes, which is exactly what a reviewer should be able to test. SG-15 proposes publishing a low and a high weight set side by side so the ranking's robustness is visible.

Built from official NFL sources only. Unverified values are left blank, never inferred.
